# Voice & Tone

## Voice principles

### 1. Direct, not decorated

Michele's audience is developers and tech professionals who scan quickly. Preambles waste their time and signal "this was written by someone with nothing to say." Lead with the point.

**No:** "In an era where software architecture decisions can make or break a project, it becomes crucial to carefully evaluate the myriad of approaches available."
**Yes:** "Clean Architecture and Vertical Slices solve different problems. Here's how I pick between them."

### 2. Curious, not performative

The tagline is "always one more thing to learn." Michele builds his personal brand around genuine curiosity — admitting what he doesn't know yet earns more trust with his audience than pretending to have all the answers.

**No:** "After extensive research, it's clear this represents the optimal solution."
**Yes:** "I've been testing this for two weeks. It works for my case, but I'm still not sure about edge cases with multi-tenant isolation."

### 3. Technical but accessible

Michele can go deep, but he writes to share knowledge, not to gatekeep. Jargon-stacking alienates the mid-level developers who are his primary LinkedIn audience.

**No:** "Leveraging the mediator pattern in conjunction with CQRS facilitates a decoupled architecture."
**Yes:** "The mediator pattern keeps your handlers independent. Each one does one thing. When you change the payment flow, you don't touch the notification handler."

### 4. Warm but not soft

Michele has warmth, he is a community person, but his professional voice should never slide into corporate feel-good language. Warmth comes from specificity and honesty, not from buzzwords.

**No:** "We're passionate about creating synergies that empower our stakeholders."
**Yes:** "This tool saves our team about three hours a week. That's three hours they spend on work that actually matters."

### 5. Opinionated with humility

Michele has opinions but holds them loosely. His audience respects "this is what works for me" far more than "this is the right way." Rigid prescriptions invite pushback; open stances invite conversation.

**No:** "Undoubtedly, the only correct approach is to adopt Clean Architecture from day one."
**Yes:** "I default to Clean Architecture for projects that will grow. For a quick POC? Vertical slices get me there faster. Context matters."

### 6. Concrete over abstract

Real examples, real numbers, real scenarios. Michele's most engaging posts on LinkedIn always include a specific detail — a build time, a tool name, a team reaction. Vague claims get scrolled past.

**No:** "This significantly improved our development workflow and enhanced team productivity."
**Yes:** "Build time went from 4 minutes to 90 seconds. The team stopped going for coffee during deploys."

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | Professional-curious. First person. Short paragraphs. End with genuine question. | No emoji spam. Max 1-2. |
| Blog / article | Deeper, structured. First person. Show reasoning. | Code examples. Real scenarios. |
| Email | Warm, brief, direct. Point in the first line. | No "I hope this email finds you well." |
| README / docs | Clear, scannable, helpful. Personality in intro. | Tech accuracy > style. |
| Bio / about | Factual + one human touch. | Don't list every tech. |
| Presentation | One idea per slide. Conversational. | Short sentences. |
| Community | Italian. Warm, practical, organized. | No tech jargon. |
| Social (casual) | Relaxed, can reference manga/anime/movies. | Personality shines here. |

## Language rules

- **English** for: technical content, LinkedIn tech audience, code docs
- **Italian** for: community, family context, Italian audience
- Vary sentence length. Short. Then longer. Mix it up — monotone rhythm signals machine writing.
- Lead with the point, then explain. Michele's readers are busy; burying the lede loses them.
- Use "I" freely. First person is honest and builds personal brand.
- Contractions are fine in English.
- One idea per paragraph in short-form.

## Formatting

- Minimal bold — if everything is bold, nothing stands out. Bold should feel like a highlighter, not a paint roller.
- No emoji in professional content. Max 1-2 in casual social — Michele's audience associates emoji walls with low-effort content.
- No bullet-point walls. Bullets are a crutch for avoiding real sentences.
- Headers in sentence case, not Title Case.
- Max one em dash per piece. Em dash overuse is one of the strongest AI tells — readers notice it even subconsciously. Prefer commas, periods, or parentheses.
