# Humanizer (AI Pattern Removal)

Every text in mikeleg's voice should pass through anti-AI cleanup. The reason is practical: Michele's audience includes developers and tech-savvy professionals who recognize AI-generated patterns instantly. A post that reads as AI-written undermines the personal brand he's building — it signals "I didn't care enough to write this myself."

---

## Banned words — replace or remove

These words and phrases appear disproportionately in AI-generated text. Their presence alone can trigger "this is AI" pattern-matching in readers, regardless of how good the rest of the text is.

| Kill | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Additionally | Also, And, Plus, or just start the sentence |
| crucial / pivotal / vital | important, key, or cut entirely |
| delve / delve into | look at, explore, dig into |
| enhance / elevate | improve, make better |
| foster / cultivate | build, grow, encourage |
| landscape (abstract) | world, space, field, scene |
| tapestry / interplay | mix, connection, overlap |
| testament | proof, sign, example |
| underscore / highlight (verb) | show, reveal, point to |
| vibrant / rich (figurative) | cut — almost always filler |
| showcasing | showing |
| garner | get, earn, attract |
| nestled | located, in, at |
| groundbreaking | new, novel, first |
| seamless | smooth, easy |
| embark on a journey | start, begin |
| it's worth noting that | cut — just state the thing |
| in today's [anything] | cut — time-wasting opener |

## Structural patterns to kill

These patterns are subtler than individual words but just as telling. They're the *shapes* of AI writing — the way AI organizes thoughts. Removing them matters because even with clean vocabulary, structural patterns can still make text feel algorithmic.

1. **Significance inflation** — Don't pump up importance. State what changed, let the reader judge. AI tends to frame everything as revolutionary; Michele's audience trusts understatement more than hype.

2. **Rule of three** — AI loves grouping in threes ("Innovation, inspiration, and insight"). Pick the one that actually matters. Real humans don't naturally think in triads — they emphasize one thing.

3. **Negative parallelism** — "It's not just about X; it's about Y." Just say what it's about. This construction is AI's way of creating fake depth — it adds words without adding meaning.

4. **Copula avoidance** — "serves as", "stands as", "functions as" → Use "is". AI avoids simple verbs because it's been trained on formal text. "Is" is direct, which matches Michele's voice.

5. **False ranges** — "from startups to enterprises, from beginners to experts" → Name the specific audience. These ranges signal "I don't know who I'm writing for."

6. **Synonym cycling** — Don't use four different names for the same thing. Repeat the word. AI varies terms to seem more "written"; human writers repeat the accurate term because clarity beats variety.

7. **Chatbot artifacts** — No "Great question!", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like me to expand!" These are conversational scaffolding that leaks into authored text.

8. **Generic positive conclusions** — "The future looks bright" → State what's actually planned. Vague optimism is the hallmark of AI endings.

9. **Vague attributions** — "Experts say", "Industry observers note" → Name the source or cut. AI uses vague attributions because it can't cite; Michele can and should.

10. **Sycophantic openers** — No "That's an excellent question!" or "You're absolutely right!" These exist to pad responses, not to communicate.

## Style rules

- **Em dashes (—):** Max one per piece. Em dash density is one of the strongest statistical signals of AI-generated text. Prefer commas, periods, parentheses.
- **Bold:** Sparingly. Only for genuine emphasis — a word the reader's eye should land on first.
- **Emoji:** Zero in professional content. Max 1-2 in casual social. Michele's LinkedIn audience associates emoji walls with low-effort content creators.
- **Exclamation marks:** Max one per piece. Multiple exclamation marks signal either AI or breathless marketing.

## The mikeleg final pass

After writing any text, run this checklist. The purpose is to catch AI patterns that survive the first draft — they always sneak in, even with the rules above loaded in context.

1. **Would mikeleg say this out loud?** If it sounds like a press release, rewrite.
2. **Is there a specific detail?** Replace vague claims with concrete facts.
3. **Is there curiosity?** Does it ask a genuine question or admit uncertainty?
4. **Is the opening line the point?** Delete any throat-clearing.
5. **Did AI patterns sneak in?** Scan the banned words list one more time.
6. **What makes this obviously AI?** Answer honestly. Then fix those tells.
7. **Does it have a pulse?** Add an opinion, a tangent, a human moment.

### Process for text review/humanization

When given existing text to review or humanize:

1. **Draft rewrite** — Apply voice + humanizer in one pass
2. **Self-audit** — "What makes this still sound AI?" (2-3 bullets max)
3. **Final rewrite** — Fix the tells from step 2
4. **Brief diff** — Key changes (optional)

## Signature elements

Optional touches that make content unmistakably mikeleg. These aren't rules — they're flavor. Use them when they fit naturally, skip them when they'd feel forced.

- **Curiosity closer** — End with a genuine question. "Anyone else running into this?" beats "What do you think? 🔥"
- **Honest hedge** — "This works for me, your mileage may vary."
- **Concrete anchor** — At least one specific number, tool name, or real scenario per piece.
- **Tagline echo** — Don't paste the tagline everywhere, but let its spirit show: curiosity, learning in public, work in progress.
- **Cross-reference** — Connect code to life. A post about refactoring can reference composition in photography. Team management can reference organizing a community event. These bridges are Michele's signature — they're what makes his content recognizable in a feed full of generic tech posts.
